Refresh Opportunity Scoring for Search Intelligence

A leakage-aware machine-learning ranking system for prioritizing pages for content-refresh review.

Abstract

Question: Can historical search and content signals rank pages that are most worth reviewing for a potential content refresh? Method: A leakage-aware Random Forest was trained on 21 raw features, including historical performance, content attributes, and April-to-May trend signals, using a client-level holdout split. Target: a refresh opportunity was defined as a page whose June impressions were at least 90% below its April–May historical impression level. Result: on the validation evaluation, the improved model achieved Precision@20 of 0.30 and Precision@50 of 0.46, compared with 0.15 and 0.22 for the hand-written baseline. Use: the output is a prioritization and decision-support system for human review, not a causal claim about why visibility changed or proof that refreshing a page will recover performance.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams can have many pages that deserve attention while review capacity is limited. This capstone addresses a practical Search Intelligence decision: which pages should be placed near the top of a content-refresh review queue?

The project uses the Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring lane. Instead of treating every declining page equally, the system combines multiple signals and produces a ranked list with reason codes and recommended actions.

Decision supported: prioritize pages for human investigation and possible refresh, while keeping the ranking explicitly directional and decision-support oriented.

Exclusions and public-safe handling

Pages without positive impressions in both the historical and June windows were excluded from the modeling set because the percentage-change target would not be meaningful for zero historical impressions or zero June impressions. Client and content identifiers are represented using hashes. No client names, domains, private queries, credentials, or raw exports are exposed here.

3. Methodology

Features

The model uses 19 numeric features and 2 categorical features. Numeric signals include historical impressions, clicks, pageviews, sessions, engaged sessions, AI sessions, average position, CTR, engagement rate, AI-session share, search volume, competition, CPC, backlinks, category count, character count, word count, and two log-transformed April-to-May change features. The categorical features are content type and main intent.

Label

is_refresh_opportunity equals 1 when impression change from the historical period to June is ≤ −90%, and 0 otherwise.

Model

The improved model is a Random Forest classifier with 150 trees, maximum depth 8, minimum samples per leaf 20, balanced class weighting, and random seed 42. Numeric values use median imputation; categorical values use most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding.

Validation and leakage control

The model uses a client-level holdout: 52 unique clients were split into 41 training clients and 11 validation clients. This prevents pages from the same client appearing across both groups. June outcome information is reserved for target construction and evaluation rather than used as a model feature.

Baseline

The hand-written baseline provides a simple rule-based comparison. Both systems are evaluated on the same validation ranking task using Precision@20 and Precision@50.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model ranks observed historical patterns; it does not establish causality.
  • The ≥90% impression-decline label is a project-defined operational definition, not a universal definition of content quality.
  • Random Forest feature importance does not prove that changing a feature will change future search performance.
  • Validation uses a client-level holdout from the available warehouse window, so performance may differ on other releases, periods, or clients.
  • Some features contain missing values and are handled through preprocessing.
Interpretation: results are observed and directional. The system is intended for prioritization and human review, not automatic content changes or claims about Google's ranking algorithm.

8. Action Playbook

High-priority refresh review

Send severe-decline pages to the front of the human review queue. Investigate the page and surrounding search/content context before deciding whether a refresh is appropriate.

Refresh review

Review strong-decline pages for stale or incomplete content, relevance, coverage, and other page-level signals before making changes.

Monitor and investigate

Keep moderate-decline pages under observation and investigate additional signals rather than automatically refreshing.

This playbook is intentionally a review workflow. It does not prescribe automatic edits or imply that a refresh will cause recovery.
